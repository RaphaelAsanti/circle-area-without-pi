Abstract

This method determines the area of a circle without using π by comparing water depths between a rectangular container and a circular container holding the same water volume. The ratio of depths directly yields the ratio of areas.

The Method

Fill a rectangular container with water, then pour that exact volume into a circular container. Measure the water depths:

  • d_rect — water depth in the rectangle
  • d_circle — water depth in the circle

The circle’s area is then:

A_circle = A_rect × (d_rect ÷ d_circle)

Generalized Form

For any two shapes filled with the same water volume:

A₂ = A₁ × (d₂ / d₁)²

Accuracy Statement

This method is exact. We use water to compare two shapes. If both shapes hold the same water, their areas match the water depths. We do not need pi at all. Pi is a number that goes on forever, so people have to round it. This method does not round anything. It uses real water and real measurements, so the answer is exact.

Why We Do Not Use Pi

Pi is a special number that goes on forever. Because it never ends, people have to round it when they use it. Rounding makes tiny errors. This method does not use pi at all. It uses water, which gives an exact answer.

Simple Example

If the rectangle has water depth 4 and the circle has water depth 2, the circle’s area is twice the rectangle’s area. This comes from the easy formula: A_circle = A_rect × (4 ÷ 2). Since we can calculate the area of the square with absolute precision we then know the area of the circle with 100% accuracy.

From One Circle to All Circles

Once we know the exact area of the first circle using water, we can calculate the area of any other circle using simple algebra. We do not need pi at all. If Circle 1 has area A₁ and Circle 2 has radius r₂, then we can compare their water depths or use ratios to find A₂ exactly. The first circle gives us a perfect reference, and every other circle follows from that reference with 100% accuracy.

You’ll Never Need Pi Again

After we measure the first circle’s area exactly using water, we can find the area of any other circle using simple ratios. Pi is only required for formulas. It is not required for measurement. Once the first circle is known, every other circle follows from that reference with perfect accuracy. You never need pi again.
