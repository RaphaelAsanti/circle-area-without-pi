Circle Area Without Using Pi

By Raphael E. Asanti

Abstract

This method determines the area of a circle without using π by comparing water depths between a rectangular container and a circular container holding the same water volume. The ratio of depths directly yields the ratio of areas.

The Method

Fill a rectangular container with water, then pour that exact volume into a circular container. Measure the water depths:

  • d_rect — water depth in the rectangle
  • d_circle — water depth in the circle

The circle’s area is then:

A_circle = A_rect × (d_rect ÷ d_circle)

Generalized Form

For any two shapes filled with the same water volume:

A₂ = A₁ × (d₂ / d₁)²

Accuracy Statement

This method is exact. We use water to compare two shapes. If both shapes hold the same water, their areas match the water depths. We do not need pi at all. Pi is a number that goes on forever, so people have to round it. This method does not round anything. It uses real water and real measurements, so the answer is exact.
